\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On-device personalization joins data sources with different trust and persistent states with different cleanup semantics. Once a received item moves from memory into adapter weights, source deletion can leave behavior behind. PoisonGuard addresses this cross-sink laundering path by preserving provenance through selection, mediating memory and training separately, quarantining candidate adapters, and recording one lineage graph across sources, derived memories, windows, audits, and checkpoints. Under explicit complete-mediation and replay assumptions, the graph supports non-laundering enforcement and counterfactual rollback-and-replay. The assumptions and costs are part of the result: socially engineered typed input, compromised runtimes, missing replay data, and nondeterministic environments remain outside the strongest guarantee. The next step is the preregistered phone evaluation in \cref{sec:experiments}. Until those runs are complete, PoisonGuard should be read as a rigorous design with testable claims, not as a measured production defense.
