\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

While our empirical prototype demonstrates effective defense on real mobile hardware and benchmarks, several practical boundaries remain.

\paragraph{Trusted Computing Base Scope.}
The TCB assumption is substantial: a compromise of the Android kernel, privileged system services, or the native training runtime breaks complete mediation. Application-level deployments can protect internal agent loops but cannot establish tamper-proof cross-application provenance without platform-level hooks.

\paragraph{Determinism and Archival Constraints.}
Exact rollback depends on deterministic execution and replay availability. Proprietary NPU/GPU accelerator kernels that introduce nondeterministic floating-point reductions, or privacy policies requiring immediate payload erasure, prevent exact bit-level parameter reconstruction. In such regimes, \PG{} gracefully degrades to checkpoint reset or bounded approximate remediation.

\paragraph{Adaptive Human Manipulation.}
Provenance distinguishes channels and authorship, but origin does not equal benign intent. An attacker who convinces a user to retype or copy-paste malicious instructions bypasses automatic untrusted-channel denial. \PG{} evaluates induced retyping to quantify this residual risk, but human social engineering requires complementary behavioral warnings.

\paragraph{Auditor Generalization.}
Pre-promotion behavioral and spectral auditors reduce risk but are not universal detectors. Highly stealthy triggers tailored to match benign gradient norms can evade pre-promotion quarantine. \PG{}'s guarantee is therefore causal rather than behavioral: when backdoors are identified, lineage rollback provides exact restoration.
